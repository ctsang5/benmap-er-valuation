* The total value to society of an individual’s avoidance of an emergency department (ED) visit is the sum of the cost of illness (COI) and the individual’s willingness to pay (WTP).  
  * For ED visits, the COI is defined as the medical costs. It’s defined differently for hospital admissions.  
  * The individual’s WTP has two components: the individual’s willingness to pay to avoid the pain and suffering from their illness; and the WTP of those involved with the ill individual (e.g., doctors, nurses, spouses, government, etc).

* Healthcare Cost and Utilization Project (HCUP) is a collection of hospital data in the United States. It contains NEDS and NIS–both of which BenMAP uses.   
* Nationwide Emergency Department Sample (NEDS) contains ED discharges.  
  * Each row is an individual discharged from an ED, their illness’s diagnosis code, and billed charges; billed charges do not include any deductions; each row also has a discharge weight–a number that says how much that row represents similar ED visits   
  * EPA pulls all ED discharges matching the cardiovascular/respiratory diagnosis code and computes the weighted national mean of billed charges (using the discharge weights)  
* National Inpatient Sample (NIS) contains hospital cost to charge ratios.  
  * Cost to charge ratio (CCR) is the cost the individual actually pays divided by the billed charge; each hospital has its own CCR  
  * EPA finds the cardiovascular/respiratory diagnosis codes in NIS data, calculates the average CCR, and applies that single average CCR to the cardiovascular/respiratory discharges in NEDS. 

* The COI of cardiovascular disease is $1,161, and the COI of respiratory diseases is $875. These do not account for wages lost while the person was sick and WTP\!
