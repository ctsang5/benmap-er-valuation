# Positioning Statement: Economic Valuation of Wildfire Smoke-Attributable ED Visits in California

---

## 1. Suggested Contribution Statement

This capstone provides the first systematic assessment of how BenMAP-CE's default cost-of-illness unit values underestimate the true economic burden of wildfire smoke-attributable emergency department visits in California. By applying Chen et al.'s (2023) epidemiological estimates of smoke-attributable ED visits to both BenMAP-CE default values and adjusted valuations that incorporate the ambulatory care gap (Birnbaum et al. 2020), work-loss underestimation (Meng et al. 2024), and the WTP-to-COI ratio (Richardson et al. 2012, 2013), this analysis demonstrates that the standard BenMAP-CE framework may capture as little as 10-15% of the true welfare cost of wildfire smoke morbidity. These findings have direct implications for California air quality policy, wildfire management resource allocation, and environmental justice considerations.

---

## 2. How This Capstone Fits Into the Literature

### Primary Literature Strand: Wildfire Smoke Economic Valuation

This capstone sits at the intersection of two mature but disconnected literatures:

1. **Wildfire smoke epidemiology** -- well-established causal evidence linking smoke exposure to ED visits in California (Chen et al. 2023; Wettstein et al. 2018; Heft-Neal et al. 2023)
2. **Environmental health valuation methodology** -- growing recognition that COI underestimates welfare costs (Richardson et al. 2012, 2013) and that BenMAP-CE has specific structural gaps (Birnbaum et al. 2020; Meng et al. 2024)

The capstone connects these strands by taking the epidemiological evidence from strand (1) and applying the methodological critiques from strand (2) to produce improved cost estimates.

### Secondary Literature Strands

- **BenMAP-CE applications**: The capstone extends the BenMAP-CE application literature (Jones 2016; Fann et al. 2018) by treating the tool not just as a valuation instrument but as an object of critique.
- **Environmental justice**: By examining how underestimation may differ across communities, the capstone connects to the growing literature on wildfire smoke disparities (Masri et al. 2021; Darling et al. 2023).

## 3. What Gap It Fills

### The Synthesis Gap

Individual papers have identified specific dimensions of BenMAP-CE underestimation:

| Dimension | Source | Magnitude of Underestimate |
|-----------|--------|---------------------------|
| WTP vs. COI | Richardson et al. 2012, 2013 | ~9x |
| Ambulatory care exclusion | Birnbaum et al. 2020 | ~40% additional |
| Work-loss undercount | Meng et al. 2024 | 5-10x |
| Differential toxicity | Aguilera et al. 2021 | 1.5-8x more harmful |
| Defensive spending | Han et al. 2024 | Not quantified in BenMAP |
| Behavioral avoidance costs | Heft-Neal et al. 2023 | Not quantified |

No existing study has assembled these individual critiques into a comprehensive assessment for a single health endpoint (ED visits) in a single state (California) during a specific period. This capstone fills that synthesis gap.

### The Applied Gap

While the Richardson et al. (2012, 2013) WTP-to-COI comparison is widely cited, it has not been applied to update economic estimates of wildfire smoke ED visit costs in California. The capstone takes a well-established finding and applies it to produce policy-relevant cost estimates.

### vs. Meng et al. (2024) -- Closest Competitor

| Dimension | Meng et al. (2024) | This Capstone |
|-----------|-------------------|---------------|
| **Health endpoint** | Work-loss days | ED visits |
| **Data source** | CHIS survey | Chen et al. (2023) ED visit estimates from OSHPD |
| **BenMAP critique** | Work-loss days underestimated 5-10x | Multiple dimensions of underestimation |
| **Valuation approach** | BenMAP-CE default vs. CHIS-derived | BenMAP-CE default vs. WTP-adjusted values |
| **Geography** | California | California |
| **Policy focus** | Work-loss policies | ED visit costs for wildfire management |

**Key differentiator**: Meng et al. focus on one dimension (work-loss) while this capstone synthesizes multiple dimensions for a different endpoint (ED visits).

### vs. Kochi et al. (2016)

Kochi et al. provide a COI-only estimate of wildfire smoke ED visit costs for a single event (2007 SoCal fires). This capstone covers a broader time period (2016-2019), a wider geography (statewide California), and critically evaluates the COI approach rather than accepting it at face value.

### vs. Connolly et al. (2024)

Connolly et al. focus on mortality valuation ($432-456B). This capstone focuses on morbidity (ED visits), which is a different and complementary piece of the total economic burden.

### vs. Fann et al. (2018)

Fann et al. apply BenMAP-CE nationally and take the default values as given. This capstone takes BenMAP-CE as the object of analysis rather than as an unquestioned tool.

## 6. Recommended Framing

### Primary Framing: Applied Policy Analysis

Frame the capstone as an applied policy analysis that asks: "When California policymakers use BenMAP-CE to value wildfire smoke health impacts, how much are they missing?" This positions the work as:
- Practically useful to state agencies (CalEPA, OEHHA, CARB)
- Building on established methods, not inventing new ones
- Contributing a synthesis that does not currently exist

### Opening Positioning (suggested structure for introduction)

1. **Establish the problem**: Wildfire smoke is an increasingly important public health threat in California, with documented impacts on ED visits (cite Chen et al. 2023; Wettstein et al. 2018).

2. **Introduce the valuation challenge**: Policymakers rely on BenMAP-CE to monetize these health impacts for benefit-cost analysis of air quality regulations and wildfire management investments (cite EPA 2023; Fann et al. 2018).

3. **Identify the gap**: However, growing evidence suggests BenMAP-CE's default cost-of-illness values substantially underestimate the true economic burden (cite Richardson et al. 2012; Birnbaum et al. 2020; Meng et al. 2024).

4. **State the contribution**: This capstone provides the first comprehensive assessment of the magnitude of underestimation for wildfire smoke-attributable ED visits in California, integrating evidence on ambulatory care gaps, work-loss undercounting, defensive expenditures, and the fundamental WTP-COI divergence.

5. **Preview the findings**: The analysis suggests BenMAP-CE may capture as little as [X]% of the true welfare cost, with implications for wildfire management resource allocation and environmental justice.

## 7. Potential Objections and Responses

### Objection 1: "The WTP estimates from Richardson et al. are from single events; they may not generalize."

**Response**: Richardson et al. (2012, 2013) study two different fire events (LA County fire and 2009 Station Fire) using two different methods (defensive behavior and contingent valuation) and obtain consistent WTP estimates ($84-95/day). The convergence across events and methods supports generalizability. Moreover, the 9:1 WTP-to-COI ratio is consistent with the broader environmental economics literature on morbidity valuation.

### Objection 2: "BenMAP-CE is the EPA standard tool; critiquing it is presumptuous for a capstone."

**Response**: The critique builds entirely on published, peer-reviewed evidence (Birnbaum et al. 2020 in Health Affairs; Meng et al. 2024 in BMJ Public Health). The capstone does not claim BenMAP-CE is wrong; it documents what the tool does and does not capture, and quantifies the implications. EPA's own Guidelines (2024) acknowledge that COI measures are known lower bounds.

### Objection 3: "You are just applying other people's adjustment factors; there is no original analysis."

**Response**: The synthesis itself is the contribution. The adjustment factors exist in isolation across different papers, endpoints, and contexts. Bringing them together for California wildfire smoke ED visits provides actionable policy information that does not currently exist. Applied synthesis is a legitimate and valuable form of scholarship.

### Objection 4: "The adjustment factors may not be additive or multiplicable."

**Response**: This is a valid concern that should be addressed transparently. The different dimensions of underestimation (ambulatory care gap, WTP-COI divergence, work-loss) overlap in complex ways. The capstone should present scenarios ranging from conservative (applying one adjustment at a time) to comprehensive (applying all adjustments), with appropriate caveats about interaction effects.

### Objection 5: "Wildfire smoke ED visit costs are tiny compared to mortality costs."

**Response**: This is true at the aggregate level (Connolly et al. 2024 estimate $432-456B in mortality costs). However, morbidity affects far more people than mortality, ED visit costs are directly measurable and policy-relevant, and the underestimation issue in BenMAP-CE affects all health endpoints including mortality. Accurate morbidity valuation is important in its own right for resource allocation and cost-benefit analysis.
