# What Is Going On: A Guide to These Files

This folder contains four literature review deliverables built to support your capstone on the economic valuation of wildfire smoke-attributable ED visits in California. Here is what each one does and how they work together.

---

## annotated_bibliography.md

**What it is:** A catalog of 34 academic papers organized into five thematic categories, each with a full citation, a 2-3 sentence annotation, and a proximity rating (1-5) indicating how directly it relates to your research question.

**How to use it:** This is your reference shelf. When you are drafting a paragraph and need to know which papers support a specific claim -- say, that BenMAP-CE underestimates work-loss costs -- scan the annotations and proximity ratings to find the right sources quickly. The five categories also suggest a natural organizational structure for your literature review sections:

1. BenMAP-CE and EPA Valuation Framework
2. COI vs. WTP Methodology
3. Wildfire Smoke Epidemiology
4. Economic Valuation of Air Pollution Health Effects
5. Environmental Justice

Papers rated proximity 4-5 are your core citations. Papers rated 2-3 provide supporting context and broader framing.

---

## frontier_map.md

**What it is:** A structural analysis of where the research field currently stands. It maps what has been established, what methods and data have been used, and -- most importantly -- where the gaps are.

**How to use it:** This document answers the question your professor will ask: "Why does your capstone need to exist?" The frontier map identifies five specific gaps in the literature, and your capstone directly fills Gap 1 (no integrated valuation critique for wildfire ED visits) and partially fills Gaps 2 and 3. The connection map at the bottom visually shows how your work bridges the epidemiology strand (Chen et al. 2023) and the economics strand (Richardson, Birnbaum, Meng). Use this to structure the narrative arc of your literature review -- walk the reader through what is known, then show them the hole your capstone fills.

---

## lit_review_report.md

**What it is:** A full structured literature review report that synthesizes all 34 papers into a cohesive narrative organized around five themes: (1) the BenMAP-CE valuation framework, (2) the COI-WTP divergence, (3) wildfire smoke epidemiology, (4) broader air pollution economic valuation, and (5) environmental justice. It also profiles the 15 most important papers individually and identifies 7 gaps and 6 concrete next steps.

**How to use it:** This is your closest-to-finished prose. While the annotated bibliography gives you paper-by-paper summaries, the lit review report weaves them together thematically -- showing how papers build on, contradict, or complement each other. Use it as a drafting scaffold: the five thematic sections can be adapted into sections of your actual literature review chapter, and the "Gaps and Opportunities" section feeds directly into your research justification. Pay special attention to the Verification Notes at the end, which flag things you need to check before finalizing (missing Stieb et al. reference, dollar-year inconsistencies, working paper caveats).

---

## positioning.md

**What it is:** A strategic document that defines your capstone's contribution, differentiates it from the four closest existing papers, assesses scooping risk, and prepares responses to likely objections.

**How to use it:** This is your argumentation playbook. It contains:

- A **draft contribution statement** you can adapt for your introduction
- A **differentiation table** showing exactly how your work differs from Meng et al. (2024), Kochi et al. (2016), Connolly et al. (2024), and Fann et al. (2018)
- A **recommended 5-step introduction structure** you can follow when drafting
- **Five potential objections with prepared responses** -- anticipating questions your professor or reviewers might raise

---

## How the Three Files Work Together

They form a pipeline:

1. **Annotated bibliography** gives you the raw materials -- what has been published, what each paper says, and how close it is to your question.
2. **Frontier map** synthesizes those materials into a landscape -- showing where the field is crowded, where it is thin, and where your capstone sits.
3. **Lit review report** weaves the papers together into thematic prose -- connecting sources to each other, identifying where findings converge or conflict, and surfacing actionable next steps.
4. **Positioning statement** turns that landscape into an argument -- here is the gap, here is why it matters, here is how your work fills it, and here is how to defend that claim.

Think of it as: **inventory -> map -> synthesis -> strategy**.

---

## What to Do Next

1. **Read the positioning statement first** -- it gives you the clearest picture of what your capstone is arguing and why.
2. **Use the frontier map's gap analysis** to outline your literature review sections. The gaps tell you where your narrative needs to arrive by the end.
3. **Use the lit review report as a drafting scaffold** -- its five thematic sections are close to the structure your actual literature review chapter needs. Adapt and refine the prose rather than starting from scratch.
4. **Check the lit review report's Verification Notes** before finalizing -- they flag the Stieb et al. gap, dollar-year inconsistencies across studies, and working paper caveats that need attention.
5. **Draft your literature review** by working through the annotated bibliography category by category, weaving sources together thematically rather than summarizing them one at a time.
6. **Keep the positioning document open** while you write your introduction -- the 5-step structure and objection responses will save you time.
